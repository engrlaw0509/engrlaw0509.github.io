The register

Four products, each built for a business that had outgrown the spreadsheet it was being run on. Status is stated plainly — what is running today, and what is still being built.

Croma MNL

In production

Specialty coffee · retail operations

The whole operating system for a Manila specialty coffee company: a point of sale, a members’ app, the public website, and a native Android shell that prints its own receipts.

163Server endpoints
14,136Lines in the POS
43Test scripts
4Connected surfaces

The point of sale covers BIR‑style invoicing, recipe‑level inventory that draws down by ingredient as drinks are made, payroll, shift handovers and Z‑readings. The Android wrapper speaks ESC/POS over Bluetooth itself, which retired a paid third‑party print service and its subscription along with it.

It is now being rebuilt on managed Postgres in Singapore. The spreadsheet backend it started on was dropping roughly one request in three on a bad afternoon, and no amount of retry logic fixes a data layer that was never meant to carry a shop floor.

Sentro

In production

Insurance · financial advisor practice management

One platform for a financial advisor’s whole practice, in three parts: the agent’s portal, a client‑facing policy portal, and an admin console for the insurer catalogue and billing.

Sold by subscription to advisors whatever insurer they write for, so the product stays insurer‑agnostic while wearing each agency’s own branding. A client can log in and see their own policies without going through their agent for a PDF.

Multi‑tenancy is structural rather than remembered. Every database read takes an authenticated scope as its first argument, and a record belonging to another agency comes back not found rather than forbidden — because a 403 confirms the record exists, and that is already a leak.

  • Next.js
  • TypeScript
  • Postgres
  • Kysely
  • Argon2id

Kaha

In development

Food & beverage · multi‑branch point of sale

A cafe point of sale built to run more than one cafe — offline‑tolerant, multi‑tenant, and configurable down to a single terminal.

One cafe can never reach another cafe’s records, and that is enforced by the database itself through row‑level security — a physical impossibility rather than a promise that every piece of code remembered its filter.

Prices are held in exact centavos with the VAT split a BIR invoice has to report, so the arithmetic on the receipt matches the arithmetic in the books. When the connection drops, sales queue on the device and settle the moment it returns — the queue closes with the till, not with the internet.

  • TypeScript
  • Hono
  • Postgres 16
  • Drizzle
  • React PWA
  • Railway

EA Builders

In production

Construction · design‑and‑build management

The system a design‑and‑build contractor runs on: projects and their critical path, the site roster, attendance that feeds payroll, materials and plant — and a portal where the client watches their own build progress.

Attendance logged on site fills the pay run directly, so a period closes without anyone re‑keying a timesheet. Payslips print six to a page, statutory deductions are set per run, and casual workers can be paid without being added to the permanent roster.

Materials carry a company catalogue, per‑site inventory and a where‑used view, so any pallet or machine can be traced to the project holding it. Each job reports its own profitability — whether it is making money, and whether it still will be at handover.

The company had no logo, no palette and no typeface when the work started. The whole visual identity was designed from nothing alongside the build.

  • Next.js 16
  • TypeScript
  • Tailwind CSS v4
02 — Services

Integration & automation

Not every problem needs a new product. A good deal of the work is making the systems a business already paid for talk to each other — and taking the manual steps out from between them.

ERP integration

Continuous one‑way sync from on‑premise and cloud retail systems into NetSuite: field mapping agreed with the client up front, a full audit trail of every record moved, and a web console they can watch it run from.

NetSuite development

SuiteScript, SDF projects, and approval workflows for purchasing, vendor bills and journals. Including a transaction‑tree tool that traces every dependency behind a record before deleting it — with deliberate limits on how far it will go, because the alternative is a very fast mistake.

Workflow & AI automation

Pipelines that carry work between the tools a team already uses, with language‑model steps where judgement is needed — reading documents, classifying, drafting. Aimed at the tasks that stayed manual only because nobody had time to automate them.

Data migration

Moving a live business off spreadsheets and onto a real database without closing for the day. Verified row by row against the original before anything is switched over, and reversible until it is.

03 — Approach

How the work is built

Three commitments that show up in every codebase above, because in this market they are not optional extras.

Compliance is a feature, not paperwork bolted on at the end.

Invoice numbering, the VAT split, Z‑readings and the 58 mm receipt layout are designed in from the first schema. Retrofitting BIR requirements onto a finished system means rewriting the parts that touch money, which is all of them.

Separation between tenants is proven, not promised.

Where several businesses share one system, isolation is enforced by the database or by types that cannot be sidestepped — and there are tests that run against a real database to demonstrate it. “Every query remembers its filter” is a hope, not a control.

It has to keep working on a bad connection.

Anything used on a shop floor assumes the network will fail mid‑transaction, because here it does. Work queues locally and settles when the line comes back, rather than showing a spinner to a customer standing at the counter with cash in hand.
